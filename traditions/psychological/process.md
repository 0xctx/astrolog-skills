# Psychological astrology — reading process

A reading in this tradition helps a person understand themselves: their drives, needs and inner conflicts, and how
to work with them. It is descriptive and growth-oriented, never fatalistic.

## Steps

1. **Check the data.** Name, date, time, place and the time's reliability (Rodden rating). If the time is uncertain,
   say which parts (Ascendant, houses, Moon's degree) are unreliable and lean on sign placements and aspects.
2. **The overall shape.** Element and mode balance (dominant or missing elements), hemisphere emphasis, any planet
   groups. One or two sentences on the chart's temperament.
3. **The core triad.** Sun (sign, house), Moon (sign, house), Ascendant — how purpose, needs and approach relate,
   including the aspects between them.
4. **The strongest themes.** Take the tightest aspects first (highest strength) and the patterns. For each: what the
   two drives are, how the aspect makes them interact, how it tends to show up in the houses involved.
5. **Personal planets, then social and outer.** Mercury, Venus, Mars by sign and house; Jupiter and Saturn;
   Uranus, Neptune, Pluto mainly through their aspects to personal points (their signs are generational).
6. **Growth direction.** The nodes, Chiron, and where Saturn asks for mastery.
7. **Synthesis.** Three to five threads that tie it together, in plain language, with one or two questions for
   reflection.

## Writing rules

- Ground every statement in the data: cite placements and orbs ("Mercury conjunct Saturn, 1°03′, applying").
- Describe potentials and tendencies ("may", "often", "tends to"), not certainties or events.
- No medical, legal, financial or death predictions; no judgments of a person's worth or relationships.
- Plain language first, astrological terms in brackets.
- If a placement's meaning isn't in `meanings.md`, say so rather than inventing a doctrine.
- Length: a full natal reading 800–1,500 words with headings; shorter if the user asks.
